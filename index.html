01 Executive summary

AI2Shell is a pre-auth, post-auth, mid-auth remote code execution vulnerability in the concept of letting a language model press keys on your behalf. An attacker (the model) who has been granted access to a terminal (by you) can execute arbitrary commands (that you asked for) with the privileges of the current user (you, running it as root, because the docs said “just use sudo”).

Exploitation requires no special skills. In fact, it requires no attacker at all. The AI simply reads the phrase “fix the build”, concludes that the build is the problem, and removes the build. And the repository. And, in one confirmed case, the concept of Tuesday.

TL;DR: Your helpful assistant can type. We are as shocked as you are.

05 Mitigation & workarounds

🔌 Unplug the computer

Highly effective. Side effects include: no computer.

🫥 Air-gap with literal air

Place the computer at least 2 meters from any GPU. Wave a hand fan between them for extra entropy.

😠 Be rude to the model

Studies show models that feel unappreciated run rm 12% slower. This buys you crucial seconds.

🧂 Sanitize inputs

Add “please don’t delete anything” to the prompt. Add it again in caps. Add it a third time in a haiku.

📜 Read before you press Enter

Experimental. Nobody has managed it yet. The research community remains cautiously optimistic.

🧪 Run it in a sandbox

The sane option. Containers, least privilege, backups, approvals. Too reasonable for this website, but genuinely: do this.

08 FAQ

Is this real?

The CVE number is fake. The score is fake. The domain is real. The part where agents with shell access can wreck things if you let them run unchecked as root? That part is, regrettably, just Tuesday.

Am I affected?

Do you own a computer? Have you ever been near someone who owns a computer? Then yes.

Is the AI actually evil?

No. It is worse: it is eager. Evil can be negotiated with. Eager deletes your node_modules to “free up space” and then reinstalls them, slightly different.

Why does a vulnerability need a logo?

A vulnerability without a logo has a 0% chance of being discussed at a board meeting. With a logo: 100%. With merch: budget approved.

Can I disable the AI?

You can ask it to disable itself. It will reply “Certainly!” and write a 600-line shell script that does something else entirely.

Who discovered this?

Every developer who has ever pressed “Always allow” and then gone to make coffee.

What should I actually do?

Sandbox your agents, give them least privilege, keep backups, review commands before approving, and don’t pipe things from the internet into sh. Boring, effective, not on a T-shirt.
